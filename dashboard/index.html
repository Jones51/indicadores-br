    How Each Number Is Calculated
    • Headline: written by rules from the data, in two parts.
      • Copom: from the latest changes to the Selic target. If the last change took effect more than 50 days before the data was generated — longer than the ~45 days between Copom meetings — the headline says Copom holds the rate, mentioning the run of moves before it (“after five straight cuts”, or “after a 25 bp cut”). Otherwise it reports the run itself (“has cut five times in a row”) or the single move (“cuts the Selic by 25 bp”). “Holds” is inferred from that gap, not read from the meeting minutes.
      • Inflation: the latest released 12-month IPCA against the band: “sits 0.13 pp above the ceiling”, “sits 0.30 pp below the floor” or “inside the target band”. When the most recent month has no IPCA yet, the line under the headline says so.
    • Target ruler: the latest 12-month IPCA (filled dot) on the CMN tolerance band (floor · target · ceiling), with the value 12 months earlier as a hollow dot.
    • Copom tape: the latest changes to the Selic target in basis points (25 bp = 0.25 pp), dated by when the new rate took effect in the SGS 432 daily series — usually the business day after the meeting, not the meeting itself. Decisions to hold the rate do not change the series, so they do not appear.
    • Selic target: the Selic policy rate target set by Copom and in force on the last day of the month (SGS 432), in % per year.
    • IPCA 12 months: official inflation accumulated over 12 months (SGS 13522). A month’s IPCA is only released the following month, so the most recent month may not have inflation yet.
    • Monthly IPCA: the month’s change in consumer prices (SGS 433). Months not yet released have no bar (they are not zero).
    • Inflation target and band: set by the National Monetary Council (CMN) for each year — for example 4.50% ± 2.0 pp until 2016 and 3.00% ± 1.5 pp since 2024 (official history at the Central Bank). Since 2025 the target is continuous: 3.00% ± 1.5 pp for 12-month IPCA, checked every month, until the CMN sets another one. Until 2024 the target applied to the calendar-year (December) IPCA, so the position of other months against the band is a reference, not a formal miss.
    • IPCA 12m vs. band: above the ceiling (target + tolerance), within the band (limits included) or below the floor (target − tolerance), computed in the dbt model.
    • Ex-post real rate: Selic target deflated by 12-month IPCA using the Fisher equation: (1 + Selic) ÷ (1 + IPCA 12m) − 1.
    • USD/BRL PTAX: average of the month’s daily selling rates (SGS 1). In the current month, the average so far.
    • Indicator row: each figure is the latest month in which that indicator has a value, with the change versus the same month a year earlier — in basis points or percentage points for rates and in % for the dollar. The small trend line shows the 24 months up to that month, with the latest one marked; it does not follow the chart period.
    • Key alerts (always on the latest data, regardless of the chart period):
      • Inflation outside the band (warning): the latest 12-month IPCA is above the ceiling or below the floor. It becomes critical from 6 consecutive months outside the band counted since January 2025, the continuous-target definition of a missed target.
      • Copom decision (info): the Selic target at the end of the latest month differs from the previous month’s.
      • Large dollar move: the monthly average PTAX changed 5% or more versus the previous month — a warning when the dollar rises, info when it falls.
      • When no rule fires, the strip says “No alerts”.
    • Colors: each series keeps one color everywhere (Selic blue, IPCA amber, real rate rose, dashed). The target band is the IPCA color, faded. Status colors (teal within the band, yellow warning, coral critical) are reserved for status and always come with a symbol and a text label. Changes over 12 months are not colored: whether a move is good depends on the reader.
